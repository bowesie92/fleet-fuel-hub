How this is calculated

The net-savings rule

A cheaper price only counts after the detour is paid for. Every saving on this site is the price gap on the gallons bought, plus the detour cost the truck avoids at the station it actually used, minus the detour cost to reach the cheaper station. Each detour cost is the extra fuel, the extra driver time and the wear on the truck. A raw price difference is never reported as a saving.

How this demo works

Everything on this site is synthetic. A generator makes a year of fuel card swipes, GPS pings and route plans for a made-up distributor with one center in Phoenix and one yard in Tucson. Station locations come from OpenStreetMap. Diesel prices are synthetic, built from the EIA weekly diesel average (benchmark source: U.S. Energy Information Administration).

What counts as savings

A saving only counts after the extra miles, the extra driver minutes and the wear on the truck are paid for. A cheaper price far away can cost more overall, and the pages say so.

About the times and units

Times are MST (Arizona, no daylight saving time). Volumes are in gallons, distances in miles and money in US dollars. Customers appear only as labels like Customer 014.

Data licences

Some public data used here comes with share-alike licences that travel with it. This is a good-faith summary of the providers' terms, not legal advice. The same text lives in DATA_LICENSES.md in the repository.

OpenStreetMap

Station locations: Data from OpenStreetMap, available under the ODbL

Station locations, and the coordinates used as stand-in customer stops, come from OpenStreetMap through the Overpass API. They form a derivative database available under the Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0.

openrouteservice

© openrouteservice by HeiGIT | Data from OpenStreetMap. Routing results licensed CC BY-SA 4.0.

Truck routes, route geometry and detour miles and minutes come from the openrouteservice API run by HeiGIT. Its results are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 and are cached and republished here under that licence.

Map tiles

The base map uses OpenFreeMap vector tiles (OpenFreeMap, © OpenMapTiles, data from OpenStreetMap), credited in the corner of every map. Tiles are loaded live and never stored here.

Everything else

The fleet, its fuel card, GPS and route plan records and every price shown are synthetic. Diesel prices are built from the EIA weekly diesel average as a benchmark, and the U.S. Energy Information Administration does not review or endorse this project.
